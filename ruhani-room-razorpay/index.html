The ritual behind the smoke

In Indian tradition, dhoop made with natural resins and herbs has long been used to purify spaces, tools and energy.

Its fragrant smoke stands for protection and gratitude. Every Ruhani Room pack carries a small ritual fact like this one, so the tradition travels with the fragrance.

How to light incense sticks and cones

  1. Choose a heat-safe holderSet it on a stable surface, away from curtains, paper and anything that can catch fire.
  2. Light the tipHold a flame to the tip for a few seconds until it catches and begins to glow.
  3. Let the flame settleGently blow it out. A glowing ember with a thin line of smoke means it is lit.
  4. Enjoy, then air the roomNever leave incense burning unattended, keep it away from children and pets, and let fresh air in afterwards.

What is the difference between incense sticks and dhoop cones?

Incense sticks are rolled around a thin bamboo stick and release a gentle, steady fragrance over a longer time. Dhoop cones have no stick: they are pressed into a cone and give a denser, richer fragrance more quickly. Choose sticks for everyday background fragrance and cones when you want a room to feel transformed fast.

Which incense fragrance should I choose?

For calm and focus, start with sandalwood. For rest at the end of the day, lavender. For festivals and guests, mogra. For a bright daytime lift, jasmine. Rose is the warm, comforting all-rounder and a favourite for gifting.

Is it safe to burn incense indoors?

Yes, with simple care. Burn incense in a heat-safe holder on a stable surface, keep a window slightly open so the room is ventilated, keep it away from curtains, children and pets, and never leave it burning unattended. If you have asthma or are sensitive to smoke, use it sparingly in a well-aired space.

How should I store incense sticks and cones?

Keep them in their packaging in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and moisture. This protects the fragrance and helps the incense light evenly every time.
